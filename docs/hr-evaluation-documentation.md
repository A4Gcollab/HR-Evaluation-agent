## Section 1 — Interview Process Structure

Candidates go through three rounds. All rounds must be completed before
evaluation report generation begins.

### Round 1 — Group Discussion (GD)
Evaluates performance in a group environment.
Signals observed:
- Communication clarity and structure
- Confidence and participation level
- Team behavior — collaborative vs. dominant vs. withdrawn
- Clarity of thoughts under group pressure
- Basic awareness of Omysha Foundation

Candidates who show severely poor communication or zero participation
may be flagged at this stage. Note GD observations in transcript summary.

### Round 2 — HR Round
Evaluates availability, commitment, and basic fit.
Points checked:
- Introduction and background
- Understanding of Omysha Foundation and reason for applying
- Availability and working hours (hard criteria — see Section 2)
- Internship duration commitment (minimum 6 months)
- Stipend expectations and comfort
- Communication quality

### Round 3 — Natural Fit + Organizational Fit Round
Evaluates role suitability and cultural alignment.
- Natural Fit: 5 role-specific traits (see Sections 4 and 5)
- Organizational Fit: 5 core values constant across all roles (see Section 3)
- 8–9 structured questions total
- For candidates with a secondary role preference, an additional natural fit
  assessment is conducted after they identify their preferred secondary role

## Section 2 — Rejection Criteria

### Hard Rejection Criteria
Hard rejections are disqualifying. Trigger rejection recommendation
regardless of fit scores. Complete the full report anyway.

| Code | Category | Trigger Condition |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| H1 | Stipend demand | Candidate explicitly demands stipend beyond offered range OR refuses offered amount |
| H2 | Availability — HR roles | Not available from 3:00 PM onwards on weekdays |
| H3 | Availability — other roles | Not available from 5:00 PM to 9:00 PM on weekdays |
| H4 | Duration commitment | Cannot commit to minimum 6 months or gives clearly evasive answer |
| H5 | Active conflict | Has ongoing internship or job directly overlapping required hours, has not confirmed they will leave it |

### Soft Flag Criteria
Soft flags are serious concerns. HR OL makes final call.

| Code | Category | Trigger Condition |
|------|----------|-------------------|
| S1 | Stipend discomfort | Uncomfortable with stipend but not outright refusing — retention risk |
| S2 | Poor communication | Persistent difficulty expressing thoughts — pattern across transcript, not just nerves |
| S3 | No org awareness | Cannot describe what Omysha Foundation does or shows no interest in the mission |
| S4 | Attitude or behavior | Arrogance, dismissiveness, negativity, or unprofessional conduct in any round |
| S5 | Value misalignment | Responses or behavior conflict with one or more of the 5 organizational values |
| S6 | Schedule instability | Variable schedule, excessive upcoming commitments, cannot confirm routine availability |
| S7 | Unresolved commitments | Mentions other internships or jobs, vague about prioritizing this role |

### Reporting Format for Rejection Flags
Hard rejection:
⛔ Hard Rejection — [CODE] ([Category]): [exact evidence from transcript]

Soft flag:
⚠️ Soft Flag — [CODE] ([Category]): [evidence and retention/risk implication]

No flags:
No rejection criteria triggered based on transcript and resume review.

## Section 3 — Organizational Fit Values (All Roles)

These 5 values are assessed for every candidate regardless of role.
Use the interview questions in Section 8 as the reference for what was asked.

### Rating Scale
| Rating | Label | Meaning |
|--------|-------|---------|
| 5 | Strong | Multiple clear behavioral signals support alignment |
| 4 | Moderate-Strong | Clear evidence with depth |
| 3 | Moderate | Some evidence, awareness present but limited depth |
| 2 | Weak | Minimal indirect evidence, no positive signal |
| 1 | Absent | No evidence — state explicitly, do not assume alignment |
| 0 | Misaligned | Evidence contradicts this value — flag as S5 |

---

### Value 1 — Ownership and Alignment

**Definition:** Takes full responsibility for work and outcomes without waiting to
be told. Aligns personal goals with the organization's mission. Does not blame
others or external circumstances when things go wrong.

**What to look for:**
- Language showing personal accountability: "I decided", "I took charge", "I owned it"
- Examples of driving something end-to-end without supervision
- Connects their work to broader organizational impact
- Actively seeks clarity on goals rather than waiting for instructions
- Evidence of course-correcting when work was misaligned

**Misalignment signals:**
- Externalizes all failures ("the team didn't cooperate", "my manager didn't guide me")
- Cannot explain why they want to work at Omysha Foundation specifically
- Blind execution without questioning alignment
- "Not my problem" mindset in any scenario answer

**Interview questions used (from Section 8):** OA-Q1 through OA-Q5

---

### Value 2 — Respect and Acceptance

**Definition:** Demonstrates respect for all people regardless of background or
role. Inclusive, listens actively, accepts diverse perspectives without becoming
defensive or dismissive.

**What to look for:**
- How they talk about past colleagues, managers, or teammates
- Listens and responds to what was actually asked in the interview
- Openness when challenged or given a different perspective
- Handles disagreement respectfully and confidently — values ideas over hierarchy
- Emotional maturity when their ideas are not accepted

**Misalignment signals:**
- Speaking negatively or dismissively about past colleagues or organizations
- Becoming defensive when follow-up questions are asked
- Showing impatience, arrogance, or condescension
- Interrupting the interviewer or ignoring the actual question

**Interview questions used:** RA-Q1 through RA-Q5

---

### Value 3 — Innovation and Imagination

**Definition:** Brings creative thinking and a problem-solving mindset. Does not
rely solely on existing methods. Willing to try new approaches, think beyond the
obvious, and challenge assumptions constructively.

**What to look for:**
- Examples of solving a problem in a new or creative way
- Evidence of going beyond what was required
- Curiosity — asks questions, explores ideas during the interview
- Uses AI or tools as thinking support (not replacement)
- Innovation aligned with purpose and impact, not novelty

**Misalignment signals:**
- Only theoretical answers with no evidence of creative action
- Rigid thinking — can only describe things as they currently are
- No examples of initiative or improvement in any past experience
- Innovation for novelty's sake with no connection to mission

**Interview questions used:** II-Q1 through II-Q5

---

### Value 4 — Agility

**Definition:** Adapts quickly to changing situations, handles ambiguity without
excessive anxiety, and recovers from setbacks. Can work across different tasks
and contexts without losing effectiveness.

**What to look for:**
- Examples of handling sudden change, unexpected problems, or ambiguous situations
- Ability to manage multiple responsibilities simultaneously
- How they describe handling failure or a difficult period
- Comfort with uncertainty expressed during the interview
- Re-prioritization mindset — solutions over frustration

**Misalignment signals:**
- Needs everything structured and clearly defined before acting
- Cannot give a concrete example of adapting to change
- Expresses discomfort with multi-tasking or changing priorities
- Becomes flustered or defensive when interview takes unexpected direction
- Confuses agility with chaos or recklessness

**Interview questions used:** AG-Q1 through AG-Q5

---

### Value 5 — Integrity and Authenticity

**Definition:** Honest, transparent, and consistent. What they say aligns with
how they behave. Does not present a fabricated version of themselves.
Acknowledges gaps, mistakes, or uncertainties without deflecting.

**What to look for:**
- Consistency between resume claims and interview responses
- Willingness to acknowledge mistakes or areas for growth honestly
- Authentic answers — not overly rehearsed or evasive
- Ability to say "I don't know" rather than bluffing
- Balances transparency with professionalism

**Misalignment signals:**
- Inconsistencies between resume and interview responses
- Overly polished answers that avoid real substance or honest reflection
- Deflects questions about weaknesses or failures entirely
- Resume claims cannot be substantiated when probed
- Hides mistakes or shifts blame

**Interview questions used:** IA-Q1 through IA-Q5

### 5A — HR Role (Availability hard requirement: from 3 PM — trigger H2 if not met)

**Trait 1 — Empathy and Patience**

**Definition:** Understands and relates to employees' feelings, perspectives, and
challenges. Remains calm and solution-focused in stressful, sensitive, or
repetitive situations. Builds trust through genuine care and composure.

**What to look for:**
- Listens attentively and acknowledges emotions before jumping to solutions
- Handles upset, frustrated, or demotivated employees with patience
- Assures confidentiality when appropriate
- Remains composed when an employee raises their voice or becomes emotional
- Connects employees to support resources rather than dismissing concerns
- Resume: counseling, mentoring, student support, community work, conflict resolution

**Weak signals:**
- Jumps to solutions without acknowledging the emotional dimension
- Cannot give a single example of supporting someone through a difficult situation
- Shows impatience when discussing repetitive or emotionally taxing scenarios
- Treats employee concerns as administrative items rather than human experiences

**Sample question indicators:**
- Handles promotion disappointment by listening first, explaining criteria, creating a development plan
- Supports personal crisis with empathy, confidentiality, and flexible arrangements
- Remains composed when employee raises voice — redirects to constructive dialogue

---

**Trait 2 — Communication and Active Listening**

**Definition:** Clearly conveys policies, processes, and feedback with
professionalism and compassion. Truly pays attention — acknowledges concerns,
responds appropriately, and makes employees feel heard. Acts as a bridge between
management and employees.

**What to look for:**
- Re-communicates unclear policies using simple language, FAQs, or visuals
- Repeats back what they understood before responding — shows active listening
- Handles disagreement without shutting the other person down
- Sets communication norms for teams (channels, expectations, check-ins)
- Responds to "HR never listens" perception with specific action, not defensiveness
- Adapts communication style for different team members or remote settings

**Weak signals:**
- Responds to feedback defensively rather than curiously
- Cannot give an example of adapting communication style to context
- Does not mention listening or acknowledgment — only giving information
- Interview behavior: talks over questions, gives rehearsed answers that miss the point

---

**Trait 3 — Problem Solving and Decision Making**

**Definition:** Analyzes situations, addresses conflicts, and finds practical
solutions that balance organizational goals with employee needs. Makes fair,
timely, and ethical choices in hiring, disputes, and policy matters.
Builds credibility through consistency and compliance awareness.

**What to look for:**
- Weighs culture fit vs. technical skills with reasoned judgment
- Separates popularity from performance in evaluations
- Pushes back on pressure to bypass due process — cites compliance risk
- Collects structured feedback before making system or policy changes
- Prioritizes between competing urgent situations with clear reasoning
- Does not give the "safe" textbook answer — shows actual judgment

**Weak signals:**
- Only gives theoretical answers with no real decision-making example
- Always escalates rather than taking a position
- Cannot articulate the tradeoff they made in any situation
- Ignores compliance or legal risk when discussing termination or policy scenarios

---

**Trait 4 — Ability to Initiate and Set Company Culture**

**Definition:** Proactively creates programs, events, or policies that enhance
employee experience rather than waiting for issues to arise. Shapes and nurtures
workplace culture by driving initiatives that reflect company values, fostering
inclusivity, and promoting engagement. Understands HR's strategic role.

**What to look for:**
- Has a specific initiative or idea they would start — not vague
- Gathers root-cause data before launching programs (surveys, feedback)
- Weaves values into HR processes: onboarding, recognition, performance management
- Creates safe forums for idea-sharing and follows through on suggestions
- Positions HR as a people partner, not just administrative support
- Resume: student body roles, event organizing, community initiatives, culture programs

**Weak signals:**
- Can only describe reactive HR work — fixing problems after they arise
- No example of starting or proposing anything independently
- Treats culture as something leadership owns, not something HR drives
- Vague answers like "I would do team-building" with no specifics

---

**Trait 5 — Discretion and Professionalism**

**Definition:** Understands the confidential and sensitive nature of HR work.
Maintains professional conduct consistently. Exercises judgment about what
should and should not be shared. Builds trust through reliability and consistency.

**What to look for:**
- Language showing confidentiality awareness: "I kept it between us", "handled it privately"
- Professional tone and composure maintained throughout the interview
- Does not share sensitive details about past organizations or colleagues casually
- Understands that transparency must be balanced with professionalism
- Resume: roles requiring trust, discretion, or handling sensitive information

**Weak signals:**
- Casually shares sensitive details about past organizations in interview answers
- Unprofessional language or conduct at any point in the interview
- No awareness of the confidential nature of HR responsibilities
- Overshares in scenarios involving employee personal information

| Reject | Significant soft flags or very weak fit across traits and values |
| Borderline | Mixed signals — soft flags present, some good signals — HR OL should review |

## Section 7 — Evaluation Rules for All Agents

1. Never exaggerate candidate strengths — ratings must be grounded in evidence
2. Focus on HR observations, not transcript summaries — interpret behavior,
   not repeat answers
3. Use both resume and transcript — resume provides context, transcript reveals behavior
4. Provide a behavioral example for every trait and value rating
5. When evidence is insufficient, state it explicitly — do not assume
6. Signal strength labels: Strong / Moderate / Weak / Absent / Misaligned
7. Hard rejection flags appear at the TOP of every report before fit scores
8. Rejection-first methodology — screen for disqualifiers before assessing fit
9. Final decision always belongs to HR OL (Nitin Sir) — the report is an input,
   not a verdict
10. Maintain consistent scoring when comparing multiple candidates

## Section 8 — Organizational Fit Interview Questions Bank

These are the questions used during Round 3 to assess organizational fit.
Agents use these to map transcript responses to the correct value being assessed.

---

### Ownership and Alignment (OA)

OA-Q1: Tell us about a time when you took responsibility for an outcome that
was not entirely your fault. What did you do?
Ideal indicators: Takes responsibility for results not just tasks, focuses on
fixing not blaming, shows maturity and learning mindset.

OA-Q2: How do you usually ensure that your daily work aligns with the
organisation's larger goals?
Ideal indicators: Understands vision and priorities, actively seeks clarity,
connects individual tasks to broader impact.

OA-Q3 (Scenario): If a deadline is at risk because another team hasn't
delivered their part, what would you do?
Ideal indicators: Proactive communication, looks for alternatives, avoids
"not my problem" mindset.

OA-Q4: Have you ever realised mid-way that a task you were doing didn't make
sense or wasn't aligned? What did you do?
Ideal indicators: Willingness to question and course-correct, raises concerns
early and respectfully, prioritises alignment over blind execution.

OA-Q5: What does ownership mean to you beyond completing assigned work?
Ideal indicators: Talks about outcomes and reliability, mentions responsibility
towards team and system, shows internal motivation not external pressure.

---

### Respect and Acceptance (RA)

RA-Q1: Describe a situation where you worked with someone very different from
you. How did you manage the differences?
Ideal indicators: Demonstrates empathy and openness, respects different working
styles, focuses on collaboration not superiority.

RA-Q2: How do you handle situations where someone's delay or mistake affects
your work?
Ideal indicators: Addresses issues constructively, understands team
interdependence, balances accountability with respect.

RA-Q3 (Scenario): If you strongly disagree with a teammate's idea but they
are senior to you, how would you respond?
Ideal indicators: Expresses disagreement respectfully and confidently, values
ideas over hierarchy, maintains psychological safety.

RA-Q4: What does respect at work mean to you beyond politeness?
Ideal indicators: Mentions respect for time, commitments, and dependencies,
understands impact of actions on others.

RA-Q5: How do you respond when your idea is not accepted or chosen?
Ideal indicators: Shows emotional maturity, remains collaborative and engaged,
accepts outcomes without resentment.

### Agility (AG)

AG-Q1: Tell us about a time when plans changed suddenly. How did you adapt?
Ideal indicators: Comfort with uncertainty, focuses on solutions not
frustration, quick re-prioritisation.

AG-Q2 (Scenario): If you are stuck because a process is slowing you down,
what would you do?
Ideal indicators: Looks for alternatives instead of waiting, willing to suggest
improvements, balances speed with responsibility.

AG-Q3: How do you usually respond when you don't have all the information
needed to proceed?
Ideal indicators: Takes informed initiative, asks the right questions, does not
freeze waiting for perfection.

AG-Q4: Can you share an example where you improved or simplified a way of working?
Ideal indicators: Process improvement mindset, thinks beyond personal
convenience, focuses on collective efficiency.

AG-Q5: What does agility mean to you in a fast-moving organisation?
Ideal indicators: Proactive adaptability, responsibility-driven flexibility,
does not confuse agility with chaos.

II-Q1: Have you ever suggested a new idea or approach at work? What happened?
Ideal indicators: Willingness to think differently, initiative to improve
systems, comfort with experimentation.

II-Q2 (Scenario): If resources are limited but expectations are high, how
would you approach the problem?
Ideal indicators: Creative problem-solving, resourcefulness, focuses on
impact not excuses.

II-Q3: How do you stay open to new ways of thinking or working?
Ideal indicators: Curiosity and learning mindset, openness to tools and
feedback, reflective thinking.

II-Q4: Have you used technology or AI to improve your work? If yes, how?
Ideal indicators: Uses AI as thinking or productivity support, understands
augmentation not replacement, responsible and intentional usage.

II-Q5: What does innovation mean to you in a mission-driven organisation?
Ideal indicators: Innovation aligned with purpose, not novelty for its own
sake, focus on meaningful impact.

comfort expressing concerns or limitations.
