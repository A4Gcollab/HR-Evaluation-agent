## Trait 1 — Structuring Work

**Definition:** Ability to break large, complex, or scattered work into clear,
sequenced components with defined milestones. Uses tools and systems to bring
clarity and visibility to project progress.

**Calibration note (HR OL) — AI tools:** Using AI tools like NotebookLM, ChatGPT,
Perplexity, or similar for planning and structuring work is a **positive
signal**, not a neutral or negative one. It reflects resourcefulness and
adaptability. Structuring work can equally happen through AI tools, team
collaboration, or personal planning systems — what matters is whether the
candidate can **organize, plan, and execute efficiently**. Do not down-score
for AI-tool use; do evaluate how effectively the tools are applied.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Has built a project plan, timeline, or roadmap — describes it specifically
- Uses tools (Jira, Trello, Notion, Sheets, NotebookLM, Excel) for tracking
- Breaks work into major areas and then into daily milestones
- Brings clarity to scattered information by creating structured artifacts
- Demonstrates end-to-end planning with concrete deliverables

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Can describe breaking work into smaller parts but without specific tools
- Comfortable coordinating tasks within teams at a general level
- Shows organizational thinking but without demonstrated planning artifacts

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Works informally without any structure or documentation
- Cannot describe a project plan they created
- No evidence of systematic structuring in any context

## Trait 2 — Anticipation & Risk Awareness

**Definition:** Identifies what could go wrong before it does. Notices gaps, quality
issues, and potential problems early and flags them to the team. Builds contingency
thinking into plans rather than reacting to crises.

**Calibration note (HR OL) — academic examples are fine:** At the intern level,
examples of catching risks in **academic/college projects** are **not
discounted** vs. professional examples. The focus is on natural fit and
instinct — does the candidate think about what could fail and anticipate it?
A strong academic example (e.g., spotting a dependency gap in a group project
before the deadline) is valid evidence for a 4. Score on clarity of thinking
and proactive mindset, not source of experience.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Can describe a specific time they caught a risk early and mitigated it
- Has a "what could fail" mindset when planning — not just optimistic
- Warns the team proactively when something seems off
- Adjusts plans proactively when signals suggest something is off-track

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Notices gaps and quality issues but relies on validation from seniors
- Can describe managing a problem but not anticipating it
- Shows awareness of risk without evidence of proactive mitigation

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Only describes managing crises after they happened
- No evidence of anticipating problems in any planning example
- Purely reactive approach to problems in all examples

## Trait 3 — Prioritization

**Definition:** Makes clear, reasoned decisions about what to work on first when
everything feels urgent. Focuses on overall project success rather than perfection
in isolated areas. Balances competing demands with practical judgment.

**Calibration note (HR OL) — "I prioritize by deadlines" is incomplete:**
Deadline-only prioritization is **basic (score ~3 at best)**. Stronger
prioritization demonstrates:
- Alignment with **organizational goals and priorities**, not just timelines
- Understanding of task **importance and impact**
- Considering **multiple factors** (effort, blockers, dependencies, stakeholder needs) beyond due dates
- **Balanced, informed** decisions rather than reactive ones

A 4+ answer shows the candidate thinks beyond timelines — weighs what matters
most for the outcome, not just what's due soonest.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Has deprioritized something — and can explain why clearly
- Prioritizes based on overall outcome, not just individual task quality
- Uses a framework or logic for deciding what matters most
- Comfortable saying "we won't do this now" and holding that position

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Responsive to urgency but largely driven by external direction
- Can describe choosing between tasks without articulating clear criteria
- Shows some prioritization but without independent judgment

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Tries to do everything — no evidence of deliberate tradeoffs
- Cannot explain the reasoning behind any past prioritization decision
- Freezes or escalates when faced with competing demands

## Trait 4 — Follow-Through

**Definition:** Ensures tasks get done — by themselves and by others. Tracks progress,
follows up, and closes loops without being reminded. Maintains quality standards
through to the final deliverable.

**Calibration note (HR OL) — team follow-through > personal for PM:**
Distinguish the two:
- **Personal follow-through** — completing one's own tasks reliably.
- **Team follow-through** — ensuring the whole team completes its responsibilities: tracking progress across members, driving accountability, closing loops that belong to others.

For a **PM role, team follow-through matters more**. A candidate who shows
strong personal reliability but no evidence of driving team accountability
should land at 3–3.5, not 4+. A candidate who describes tracking and unblocking
others, following up across members, and driving overall completion is the
4+ signal.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Has a system for tracking what's pending and what's overdue
- Keeps refining work until it meets a strong standard
- Compares final output with market or competitor quality
- Follows up on others without being asked; closes projects properly

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Strong focus on monitoring and ensuring task completion within timelines
- Completes own tasks reliably but limited evidence of tracking others
- Shows follow-through on personal commitments without systematic tracking

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Relies on others to remind them of pending items
- Projects described always trail off — no clear closure
- No evidence of systematic follow-through in any context

## Trait 5 — Analytical Thinking

**Definition:** Breaks complex problems into manageable components. Practical and
process-aware. Can simplify complicated situations by identifying the core
structure and creating actionable sub-tasks.

**Calibration note (HR OL) — intern-level minimum vs. strong:**
- **Minimum (score ~3):** candidate can break down tasks and think in a
  structured way. Basic decomposition is present.
- **Strong (score 4+):** candidate goes further — identifies **dependencies
  between tasks**, understands how parts connect, thinks through execution
  logically, and shows **ownership and independent thinking** (not constantly
  asking for guidance on every step).

Analytical thinking also shows up in how the candidate **drives their own
clarity** without relying on the interviewer to structure the problem for them.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Simplifies complex projects by identifying core components and dependencies
- Divides work into logical categories (e.g., hardware, software, cloud)
- Creates daily goals from higher-level project objectives
- Demonstrates structured decomposition with specific examples

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Practical and process-aware but dependent on guidance in complex situations
- Can describe analysis at a general level without demonstrated decomposition
- Shows some analytical thinking without deep structural insight

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Jumps to solutions without mapping the problem
- Cannot describe how different parts of a system connect
- No evidence of breaking down complexity in any example
