# Natural Fit Subskill — Product Management Role

> Source: docs/hr-evaluation-documentation.md Section 5D

---

## Trait 1 — Systems Thinking

**Definition:** Sees the whole picture — understands how different parts of a product,
team, or process interact and affect each other.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Describes a problem by mapping its components before proposing a solution
- Understands dependencies between teams, timelines, and features
- Can explain a product decision in terms of its downstream effects
- Demonstrates holistic thinking about interconnected systems

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Shows awareness that things are connected but without mapping dependencies
- Can describe a system but without demonstrating how they think about interactions
- Some evidence of looking beyond their immediate scope

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Jumps to solutions without mapping the problem
- Cannot describe how different parts of a system connect
- Thinks in isolated tasks, not interconnected systems

---

## Trait 2 — User Empathy

**Definition:** Keeps the end user at the center of every decision. Understands their
needs, pain points, and behavior through observation and research.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Has spoken to or observed users to understand their experience
- Describes product decisions from the user's perspective, not just business
- Can articulate a specific user problem they helped solve
- Shows evidence of user research informing decisions

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Mentions users without specific evidence of understanding their needs
- Shows awareness that user perspective matters but limited depth
- Can describe a user problem in general terms but not from observation

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Focuses only on features or technical requirements, not user experience
- Cannot describe what a specific user type needs or struggles with
- No evidence of user-centered thinking in any context

---

## Trait 3 — Prioritization and Tradeoff Thinking

**Definition:** Can make clear, reasoned decisions about what to build, fix, or defer
when everything feels urgent.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Has deprioritized something and can explain why clearly
- Uses a framework or logic for deciding what matters most
- Comfortable saying "we won't do this now" and holding that position
- Demonstrates tradeoff articulation in past decisions

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Can describe making choices but without articulating the tradeoff
- Shows awareness that not everything can be done at once
- Some prioritization evidence without a systematic approach

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Tries to do everything — no evidence of deliberate tradeoffs
- Cannot explain the reasoning behind any past prioritization decision
- No evidence of making hard choices about what matters most

---

## Trait 4 — Communication and Alignment

**Definition:** Keeps cross-functional teams aligned. Communicates product decisions,
context, and changes clearly to different audiences.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Can adapt communication for engineers, designers, and business stakeholders
- Documents decisions and shares context proactively
- Has aligned a team around a change that some people resisted
- Demonstrates multi-directional communication skill

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Communicates clearly but primarily in one direction or to one audience
- Shows awareness of different stakeholders without demonstrated adaptation
- Can share updates but limited evidence of seeking input

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Works well in isolation but struggles to coordinate across teams
- Communication is one-directional — shares updates but does not invite input
- No evidence of cross-functional communication ability

---

## Trait 5 — Bias Toward Action

**Definition:** Moves things forward despite ambiguity. Makes reasonable decisions with
incomplete information rather than waiting for perfect clarity.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Has shipped, launched, or started something with imperfect information
- Comfortable with iteration — does not need everything finalized before beginning
- Takes initiative when the path is unclear
- Demonstrates action-first, learn-fast thinking

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Shows willingness to act but prefers more information first
- Can describe starting something but without ambiguity tolerance signals
- Some initiative evidence without demonstrated comfort with uncertainty

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Waits for full specifications before starting anything
- All examples involve being directed rather than self-initiated
- No evidence of acting under uncertainty in any context
