# Natural Fit Subskill — HR Role

> Source: docs/hr-evaluation-documentation.md Section 5A
> Hard requirement: Available from 3 PM onwards — trigger H2 if not met.

---

## Trait 1 — Empathy and Patience

**Definition:** Understands and relates to employees' feelings, perspectives, and
challenges. Remains calm and solution-focused in stressful, sensitive, or repetitive
situations. Builds trust through genuine care and composure.

**Calibration note (HR OL) — correct approach without an example = 3 to 3.5:**
If a candidate gives the right *theoretical* approach ("listen first, then
act", "acknowledge emotions before solving") but **does not back it with a
real-life example**, cap the score at 3 to 3.5:
- **Score 3** — basic conceptual answer, correct but without evidence of application
- **Score 3.5** — same conceptual answer with some depth or clarity (e.g.,
  explains *why* listening first matters, links it to stress-handling), but
  still no concrete example

To earn 4+, the candidate must show **demonstrated application** through a
real experience — supporting someone through a tough situation, handling a
repetitive concern without losing patience, etc. A real example is what
converts theoretical understanding into credible empathy.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Listens attentively and acknowledges emotions before jumping to solutions
- Gives specific examples of supporting someone through a difficult situation
- Remains composed when describing high-stress or emotionally taxing scenarios
- Resume shows counseling, mentoring, student support, community work, or conflict resolution
- Connects employees to support resources rather than dismissing concerns

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Shows general awareness of emotional dimensions but limited depth
- Can describe a supportive situation but without specificity
- Acknowledges the importance of patience without strong behavioral evidence

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Jumps to solutions without acknowledging emotional dimension
- Cannot give a single example of supporting someone
- Shows impatience when discussing repetitive or emotionally taxing scenarios
- Treats employee concerns as administrative items

## Trait 2 — Communication and Active Listening

**Definition:** Clearly conveys policies, processes, and feedback with professionalism
and compassion. Truly pays attention — acknowledges concerns, responds appropriately,
and makes employees feel heard. Acts as a bridge between management and employees.

**Calibration note (HR OL):** Evaluate this trait from **both** sources equally —
(a) how the candidate *answers* questions about communication scenarios (clarity,
structure, articulation), AND (b) how they *behave* during the interview itself
(listening carefully, responding on-point, staying engaged). A candidate who
gives great scripted answers about communication but talks over questions or
drifts off-topic in-interview should be scored lower, not higher.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Re-communicates unclear policies using simple language, FAQs, or visuals
- Repeats back what they understood before responding — shows active listening
- Handles disagreement without shutting the other person down
- Sets communication norms for teams (channels, expectations, check-ins)
- Adapts communication style for different team members or remote settings

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Communicates clearly but does not demonstrate adaptation to context
- Listens to questions and responds relevantly but without depth
- Adequate clarity without standout communication skills

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Responds to feedback defensively rather than curiously
- Cannot give an example of adapting communication style
- Talks over questions or gives rehearsed answers that miss the point
- Does not mention listening or acknowledgment — only giving information

## Trait 3 — Problem Solving and Decision Making

**Definition:** Analyzes situations, addresses conflicts, and finds practical solutions
that balance organizational goals with employee needs. Makes fair, timely, and ethical
choices in hiring, disputes, and policy matters.

**Calibration note (HR OL):** The ideal is a **balance of structured thinking
and practical instinct, with a slight preference for practical/instinct-based
problem-solving** — that's how candidates will actually operate on the job.
Pure framework recitation without applicability scores lower. Pure gut-feel
without any logical flow also scores lower. The strongest answers combine
practical applicability with visible reasoning (even a loose structure counts).

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Weighs culture fit vs. technical skills with reasoned judgment
- Pushes back on pressure to bypass due process — cites compliance risk
- Collects structured feedback before making system or policy changes
- Prioritizes between competing urgent situations with clear reasoning
- Shows actual judgment, not "safe" textbook answers

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Can describe a decision but without articulating the tradeoff
- Shows awareness of multiple factors without demonstrating structured analysis
- Adequate problem-solving without standout analytical depth

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Only theoretical answers with no real decision-making example
- Always escalates rather than taking a position
- Cannot articulate the tradeoff in any situation
- Ignores compliance or legal risk in policy scenarios

## Trait 4 — Ability to Initiate and Set Company Culture

**Definition:** Proactively creates programs, events, or policies that enhance employee
experience rather than waiting for issues to arise. Shapes workplace culture by driving
initiatives that reflect company values.

**Calibration note (HR OL):** For **intern-level candidates**, the bar is
awareness and understanding of culture — NOT fully-formed culture programs.
Specifically:
- It is sufficient if they understand HR plays a role in shaping and maintaining culture, and show basic awareness of team dynamics, collaboration, and work environment.
- Having concrete program ideas is a **positive addition** but **not mandatory** for 4 or 5.
- Evaluate on *how they think about culture* and *how realistically they express understanding* — not whether they've launched anything.

Do not score a candidate down to 2 just because they haven't designed a
culture program. Do score down if they show no awareness of culture as a
topic at all.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Has a specific initiative or idea they would start — not vague
- Gathers root-cause data before launching programs (surveys, feedback)
- Weaves values into HR processes: onboarding, recognition, performance management
- Positions HR as a people partner, not just administrative support
- Resume: student body roles, event organizing, community initiatives

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Mentions the importance of culture but with limited specific ideas
- Can describe participating in culture initiatives but not driving them
- Shows awareness of HR's strategic role without proactive examples

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Can only describe reactive HR work — fixing problems after they arise
- No example of starting or proposing anything independently
- Treats culture as something leadership owns, not HR
- Vague answers like "I would do team-building" with no specifics

## Trait 5 — Discretion and Professionalism

**Definition:** Understands the confidential and sensitive nature of HR work. Maintains
professional conduct consistently. Exercises judgment about what should and should not
be shared.

**Calibration note (HR OL) — past-organization disclosures:** If a candidate
shares **internal problems from a previous organization** during the interview,
treat it as a **potential red flag** for discretion — but judge by *how* they
share, not just that they share:
- **Red flag (score 1–2):** candidate names specific people, shares confidential details, or dwells on sensitive internal issues. Signals lack of professional judgment.
- **Acceptable (score 3–4):** candidate discusses challenges in a **general, respectful, non-sensitive** way, focusing on learnings rather than specifics.
- Score on **tone, intent, level of detail, and whether they maintain professional boundaries** — not on whether the anecdote exists at all.

**Score 5 indicators:**
- Language showing confidentiality awareness: "kept it between us", "handled privately"
- Professional tone and composure maintained throughout interview
- Does not share sensitive details about past organizations casually
- Understands transparency must be balanced with professionalism
- Resume: roles requiring trust, discretion, or handling sensitive information

**Score 3 indicators:**
- Generally professional conduct without standout discretion signals
- Does not overshare but also does not demonstrate active confidentiality awareness
- Maintains appropriate boundaries without explicitly articulating why

**Score 1 indicators:**
- Casually shares sensitive details about past organizations in answers
- Unprofessional language or conduct at any point
- No awareness of confidential nature of HR responsibilities
- Overshares in scenarios involving employee personal information
